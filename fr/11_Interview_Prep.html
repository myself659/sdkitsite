67 Questions Réelles d'Entretien et Critères d'Évaluation ​

Total des Éléments: 67

#CatégorieQuestion PoséeCe que les recruteurs évaluent réellementNiveau
1ProcessDesign [X]. You have 45 minutes.Structure above all: requirements, estimation, high level, deep dive, wrap up. Running out of time is the top failure.Core
2ProcessWhat are the functional requirements?You should ask this in the first two minutes, unprompted.Foundation
3ProcessWhat scale are we designing for?Users, QPS, data volume. Every decision downstream depends on the answer.Foundation
4ProcessEstimate the storage this needs per year.Back-of-the-envelope maths with stated assumptions. Being roughly right is enough.Foundation
5ProcessWhy did you choose that database?Every component invites this. An unjustified choice is a lost point.Core
6ProcessWhat is the bottleneck in your design?Self-awareness. Usually the database. Say so before they do.Core
7ProcessWhat happens when this component fails?Every box you draw should have a failure story.Core
8ProcessHow would you know if this system was broken right now?Observability. Most candidates never mention monitoring at all.Core
9FundamentalsExplain CAP theorem, then tell me where your design sits.Applying theory rather than reciting it.Core
10FundamentalsStrong or eventual consistency here? Justify it.Product judgement, not just technical preference.Core
11FundamentalsWhat is the difference between latency and throughput?Basic but frequently muddled under pressure.Foundation
12FundamentalsHow do you scale this from 1,000 to 10 million users?The staged scaling narrative: cache, replicas, shards, services.Core
13FundamentalsWhy not just use a bigger server?Vertical scaling limits and cost curves. Sometimes the answer is yes.Core
14DatabasesSQL or NoSQL for this? Defend your choice.Judgement. Defaulting to NoSQL for scale without reasoning is a red flag.Core
15DatabasesHow would you shard this data? What is your partition key?The most consequential decision in the design.Advanced
16DatabasesYour partition key is user ID. What about the top 1% of users?Hot partitions. The follow-up that catches most candidates.Advanced
17DatabasesHow do you add a shard without moving all the data?Consistent hashing.Advanced
18DatabasesA user updates their profile and sees the old version. Why?Replication lag and read-your-writes consistency.Advanced
19DatabasesHow do you handle a schema change on a live 500 million row table?Expand-contract migration. A real operational question.Advanced
20DatabasesTwo users buy the last item simultaneously. Walk me through it.Transactions, locking, optimistic concurrency.Core
21CachingWhere would you add caching in this design?Multiple layers: browser, CDN, application, database.Core
22CachingHow do you invalidate the cache?The genuinely hard problem. TTL, write-through, event-driven invalidation.Advanced
23CachingA hot key expires and the database falls over. Prevent it.Cache stampede: locking, probabilistic early refresh, staggered TTLs.Advanced
24CachingWhat is your cache hit rate, and what happens if it drops?Whether you thought about the cache-miss path capacity.Advanced
25ScalingThe load balancer is a single point of failure. Fix it.Redundant balancers, DNS failover, anycast.Core
26ScalingHow does a new server start receiving traffic?Service discovery, health checks, registration.Core
27ScalingTraffic is 10x forecast. What breaks first?Reasoning about your own design's weakest link.Advanced
28ScalingWhat do you turn off first under extreme load?Graceful degradation and load shedding. Rarely mentioned unprompted.Advanced
29MessagingWhy put a queue here rather than calling directly?Decoupling, buffering, retry. And when a direct call is correct.Core
30MessagingHow do you guarantee a message is processed exactly once?Honestly: at-least-once delivery plus idempotent consumers.Advanced
31MessagingThe consumer crashes mid-batch. What happens?Offset commit strategy and duplicate handling.Advanced
32MessagingA message fails repeatedly. Where does it go?Dead letter queues, retry limits, alerting.Core
33MessagingHow do you preserve ordering while processing in parallel?Partition by key. Ordering within a partition, not globally.Advanced
34ReliabilityThe downstream service is slow. What does the user see?Timeouts, circuit breakers, fallbacks. Not an infinite spinner.Core
35ReliabilityHow do you prevent one slow dependency from taking down your service?Bulkheads, timeouts, circuit breakers, thread pool isolation.Advanced
36ReliabilityDesign a rate limiter for this API.A standalone question in its own right. Know token bucket cold.Core
37ReliabilityAn entire region goes down. What is your plan?Multi-region, RTO and RPO, failover mechanics, data replication.Advanced
38ReliabilityHow do you know your failover actually works?Chaos engineering and game days. Untested failover does not work.Advanced
39Real-timeHow does the client learn about a new message?Polling, long polling, SSE or WebSockets, with the trade-offs.Core
40Real-timeYou have 10 million persistent connections. How do you manage them?Connection servers, service discovery, horizontal scaling of the WS layer.Advanced
41Real-timeThe user is offline when the message arrives. Now what?Message store, push notifications, delivery on reconnect.Core
42SecurityHow is a request authenticated across your services?Gateway validation, token propagation, service-to-service auth.Core
43SecurityWhere does the token get validated, and by whom?Gateway versus each service, and the trade-off between them.Core
44SecuritySomeone floods your API with requests. Respond.Rate limiting, WAF, DDoS protection, cost controls.Core
45SecurityThis data is subject to GDPR. What changes in your design?Data residency, deletion, encryption, audit. Architecture is affected.Advanced
46ObservabilityHow would you debug a slow request across eight services?Distributed tracing and correlation IDs.Core
47ObservabilityWhat is your SLO for this system?Turning reliability into a number with consequences.Advanced
48ObservabilityWhat do you alert on, and what do you deliberately not alert on?Alerting on symptoms rather than causes; avoiding alert fatigue.Advanced
49CostWhat does this system cost to run per month?Commercial awareness. Very few candidates can even estimate.Advanced
50CostYour storage bill doubled. Investigate.Retention, tiering, replication factor, unbounded log growth.Advanced
51CostHow would you reduce this cost by half without hurting users?Caching, tiering, compression, right-sizing, reserved capacity.Advanced
52Trade-offsShould this be microservices or a monolith?Usually a monolith for a small team. Saying so scores points.Core
53Trade-offsWhy not use Kafka here?Recognising when a simpler queue or a direct call is correct.Advanced
54Trade-offsWhat would you do differently with 10x the budget? With half?Whether your design is a considered choice or the only one you know.Advanced
55Trade-offsWhat is the simplest thing that would work?Judgement. Over-engineering is as much a failure as under-engineering.Core
56Low Level DesignDesign a parking lot system.The most asked LLD question, especially at Indian companies.Core
57Low Level DesignDesign an elevator system.State machines and scheduling in object form.Core
58Low Level DesignDesign BookMyShow or a movie ticket booking system.Concurrency and seat locking at the class level.Advanced
59Low Level DesignDesign Splitwise.Data modelling for balances and settlement.Core
60Low Level DesignDesign a vending machine.The classic state pattern question.Core
61Low Level DesignDesign a notification system with multiple channels.Strategy and factory patterns applied cleanly.Core
62Low Level DesignWhich SOLID principle does this design violate?The standard framing for LLD feedback.Core
63Low Level DesignIs this design thread-safe? Make it so.Concurrency at the object level.Advanced
64PracticeWalk me through the architecture of a system you have built.Your own work, which is where senior interviews actually go.Core
65PracticeWhat is the hardest technical decision you have made?Judgement and ownership. Prepare a real story with a trade-off.Core
66PracticeWhat would you change about a system you worked on?Self-awareness and design maturity.Core
67PracticeExplain this architecture to a non-technical stakeholder.Communication, which decides senior hiring more than depth does.Core
